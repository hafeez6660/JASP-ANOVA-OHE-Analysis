Exploring the Impact of Context on the Outgroup Homogeneity Effect Using JASP ANOVA and Regression Analysis   Abstract This study investigates the Outgroup Homogeneity Effect (OHE) by applying the study with a 2x2 factorial design by manipulation of the context (Alone vs. Compare) and target population (ingroup vs. outgroup). The OHE adds that people see outgroups as homogeneous than ingroups with the influence that contextual cues have in suppressing this effect being context dependent. In particular, the theoretical basis of relating the effects that these contextual manipulations have on the perceived homogeneity of ingroups and outgroups will be Self-Categorization Theory (SCT). Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was used and Linear Regression were used to test this hypothesis. The ANOVA has shown that there is significant main effect of context whereby, the participants had higher homogeneity rating of the outgroup in the Alone condition than in Compare condition. The key impact of target group was however, not significant implying that the ingroup and outgroup were not thought differently without considering the context. There was a high interaction between the context and the target group and therefore, the interaction of the two factors was critical in the interpretation of perceptions towards homogeneity. Linear regression model supported the results of ANOVA and found that the Context and the interaction between the Context and Target are significant predictors of perceived homogeneity and the model predicted 11.3% of the variance in the dependent variable. The results herein underscore the dynamic nature of group perception and how the comparative context contributes towards the perception of groups. The paper itself adds to the insight into the natural processes existing in groups in relation to the intergroup processes providing the empirical evidence on the applicability of the SCT as well as giving the directions on the way further studies should be produced on the contextual factors in social placements.   1. Introduction Outgroup Homogeneity Effect (OHE) is the tendency at which people describe outgroups as being more homogeneous as compared to ingroup. This effect has been attributed to Self-Categorization Theory (SCT) that indicates that since there is an intergroup context, people factor outgroups based on their common traits but when there is an intragroup context, they factor ingroup based on their heterogeneous traits. This paper is a test of the hypothesis which is: Hypothesis: The outgroup will be rated, in the alone condition, as more homogeneous than the ingroup and, in the Compare condition, they will rate an ingroup and outgroup homogeneous as similar. Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) and Linear Regression tests were done to test this hypothesis. The studies involved in these analyses were on the effects of context (Alone vs. Compare) and target group (ingroup vs. outgroup) on the perception of the group homogeneity.   2. Method 
2.1. Participants and Design The experiment consisted of a 2x2 design that was a factorial type of study, with two independent variables that were manipulated: 
2.1.1. Context (ContextF): The context under which the ratings were done, which consisted of two levels: Single User: The ratings of perceived homogeneity of ingroups and outgroups were made separately. Compare: The perceived homogeneity of overscored ingroup and outgroups, relative to each other, were compared by the participants. 
2.1.2. Target Group (TargetF): The rated group, of which there are two levels: Ingroup (Australians) Outgroup (Americans) The participants were requested to give their estimates of the proportion of an individual in each group (ingroup vs. outgroup), who displays specific traits. Perceived homogeneity, or average percentage of group members displaying the traits was used as the dependent variable to measure.   3. Analytical Approach The Two statistical methods were used to find the analysis of the data: 
3.1. ANOVA The aim of ANOVA is to check the main effects (of Context and Target) and their interaction. 
3.2. Linear Regression To investigate how Context and Target contribute to the perceived homogeneity, and also to investigate the interaction between context and Target.   4. Results

Discussion This research has shown that the Outgroup Homogeneity Effect (OHE) is significant in that context is a critical factor in the perception individuals have of group homogeneity. The OHE was evident in the Alone condition where the out-group was perceived to be more homogenous when compared to the ingroup. But in the Compare condition, this effect was nullified and the ingroup and outgroup were assessed as equally homogeneous, which can be explained by the prediction of Self-Categorization Theory (SCT). The interaction effect of context and the target is big to indicate that the comparative context affects homogeneity of ingroup and outgroups in different ways. These findings were also supported by the regression analysis which revealed that the context as well as the interaction term played a significant role in the model.   7. Conclusion This study supports the fact that the context moderates the Outgroup Homogeneity Effect. The high effect of the context and target group indicates that perceptions of homogeneity of ingroups and outgroups are same when groups are directly compared. The discovery presents the significance of the context in instigating the way people imagine group homogeneity and it indicates the usefulness of Self-Categorization Theory in explaining this phenomenon.   8.
